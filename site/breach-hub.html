What do we patch first?

Thousands of vulnerabilities (CVEs) are published every week, and fewer than 1 in 250 is ever attacked. We have to predict which ones will be. We hand in 500 CVEs from 2026, and the judges count how many made it onto CISA's KEV list, the official list of confirmed attacks.

CVSS says how bad an attack would be. It doesn't say whether anyone will attack. That's our opening: combine exploit signals, description text, who published the CVE and historical patterns to find what CVSS and EPSS miss.

Quick glossary

CVE
Unique ID for a vulnerability, e.g. CVE-2024-3400. The year in the ID isn't always the publish year.
NVD / NIST
The US database that publishes and analyzes CVEs. Source of vulnerabilities, products and references.
KEV
CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities: CVEs with confirmed attacks. The answer key.
CVSS
Severity score from 0 to 10. Measures potential damage, not the chance of attack.
EPSS
Probability (0–1) that a CVE is attacked in the next 30 days. FIRST's model; it changed in March 2025.
CWE
Weakness type, e.g. CWE-79 (XSS) or CWE-89 (SQL injection).
CPE
Standard product name (vendor + product + version) NIST assigns during analysis.
PoC
Proof of concept: demo code showing how to exploit the flaw.
Exploit-DB / Metasploit
Public archive of exploits / attack toolkit with ready-to-run modules.
Nuclei
A scanner. A Nuclei template lets anyone find vulnerable systems at scale.
Assigner (CNA)
Who assigned the ID, usually the vendor's security team or a researcher.
snapshot_month
The month we predict from. Nothing after the end of that month counts.

Rules and traps

The official rules are few. The data traps are many, and nearly all come down to one thing: the NIST files are today's versions, not snapshots from prediction time.

Challenge rules

  • Predict only with what was known at the end of the snapshot_month.A March CVE can't use signals from April onward.
  • Submit top500.csv with a single column, cve_id.500 rows. Judges count how many joined KEV through Nov 4, 2026.
  • Use at least one IBM technology.Granite, Granite TimeSeries, watsonx.ai, watsonx Orchestrate, Red Hat AI Inference Server or vLLM.
  • State in the write-up which columns we used.Judges want to see we didn't lean on columns that leak the answer.
  • Devpost:2–3 min video, repo, write-up, links to outside data and the top 500.

Data traps

  • NIST reviews attacked CVEs first.So nvd_status, n_affected_cpes, n_references, blank scores and news links give the answer away. In our backtest, 100% of positives are "Analyzed" vs 50% of negatives.
  • last_modified is mid-2026 or later on every row.Useless as a signal, and it's after the snapshot anyway.
  • June 2026 has no signals.Those CVEs use July as their snapshot_month.
  • EPSS changed models in March 2025.Don't mix raw values from before and after. Use the percentile within each month.
  • February 2025 is incomplete.About 14,500 CVEs are missing from exploit_signals_2025.csv.
  • ~11,800 IDs in the signal files aren't in vulnerabilities.csv.They're rejected IDs. They drop out on join, which is fine.
  • 62 CVEs were added to KEV before their publish date.Watch for this when building date-based labels.
  • The ID year isn't the date.CVE-2024-13159 was published in 2025. Use published.
  • The answer window is uneven.A January 2026 CVE had ~10 months to reach KEV; a September one, ~5 weeks.
  • Not on KEV doesn't mean "not attacked".KEV only holds attacks CISA confirmed.
  • NIST hasn't reviewed 51,046 of the 92,205 CVEs from 2026.Half the scoreboard has no product list. Every CVE has a description.

Questions we already asked

Does "CVSS found 7" mean the top 7 are already labeled?

No. They sorted all 72,670 CVEs by CVSS, took the top 500, and 7 of those ended up on KEV. We don't know which ones or where they ranked. It's the bar to beat, not a hint.

Where is the 2026 answer?

Only the judges have it (KEV through Nov 4, 2026). known_exploited.csv stops at Dec 31, 2025: we use it to build labels for 2024–2025 and train on those.

What's a "public signal"?

Evidence anyone can see that someone is already working on attacking the CVE: high EPSS, an Exploit-DB exploit, a Metasploit module, a Nuclei template or a GitHub PoC. Note: only 42% of backtest positives had any flag.

Why do CVSS and EPSS score so low?

CVSS measures damage, not attacker interest: there are thousands of 9.8s in plugins nobody attacks. EPSS does measure probability, but in 2026 it's squashed (median 0.0018), and the easy CVEs already joined KEV before month-end, so they were dropped from the scoreboard.

Strategy and techniques

The core idea: test every idea on a backtest that mirrors the scoreboard with 2025 data before touching 2026. If it doesn't raise top-500 hits there, it doesn't go in.

Pipeline

  1. Test benchRun analysis/backtest_2025.py. It gives 35,281 labeled 2025 CVEs and the baselines. Every idea is scored as hits in the top 500.
  2. Leak-free labelsOne cohort per month: published that month and not on KEV at month-end. y = 1 if it joins KEV later. Add 2024 cohorts to get more positives.
  3. FeaturesSignals, attack shape, text, assigner, clusters of sibling CVEs, time to cutoff. See the table.
  4. ModelLogistic regression as the reference, then LightGBM with class weights or LambdaRank. Ensemble by percentile if it helps.
  5. Temporal validationTrain on older months, test on newer ones. Never a random split. Report hits@500 per month.
  6. 2026 ranking + explanationScore the scoreboard, export the top 500 and, for each CVE, the signals that weighed most plus a sentence written by Granite.
  7. Write-upTable of columns used with their traffic light, the backtest, and the comparison with baselines.

IBM technology and where it fits

Text is the only signal that's complete and leak-free for all 72,670 CVEs in 2026. That's where Granite adds real value, not just a checkbox.

Granite Embedding

Recommended

Turns each description into a vector. Use it for technology clusters, similarity between sibling CVEs and model features.

e.g. granite-embedding-30m-english (fast) or the multilingual version
huggingface.co/ibm-granite

Granite Instruct (LLM)

Recommended

Reads the description and answers in JSON: vendor, product, device type (VPN, firewall, CMS, browser…), unauthenticated?, RCE? Also writes the reason for each CVE in the top 500.

check exact Granite instruct model names on Hugging Face
huggingface.co/ibm-granite

Granite TimeSeries (TTM)

Optional

Forecasts short series, like a CVE's monthly EPSS path in 2024–25. Limit: each 2026 CVE has a single data point (its snapshot), so it helps analysis more than the ranking.

granite-timeseries-ttm-r2
